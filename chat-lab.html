Bhippi AIChat redesign proposal

Orange means Bhippi is working. Blue means it's your move.

A new look and feel for the Bhippi AI panel. The logo itself becomes the loader, every step gets a round status circle, the timeline shows edits as they happen, and scrolling keeps your place the way ChatGPT and Codex do. The panel on the right is a working prototype, not a picture.

Speed
Scroll
  1. 1Send. Your message rises out of the composer in 280ms. No rocket, sparks or smoke.
  2. 2Anchor. Your message glides to the top of the panel and the reply gets reserved room below it.
  3. 3Think. The logo's two halves open and turn. A small window shows the reasoning, then folds to "Thought for 5s".
  4. 4Work. Each step gets a circle and a plain-English label. The timeline strip shows the pauses being cut.
  5. 5Fold. Finished steps collapse into one line so the answer comes first.
  6. 6Answer. Words fade in. An orange dot marks where text is arriving.
  7. 7Summary. What changed, before and after, and one button to undo the whole turn.

Scroll up inside the panel while it works: it lets go straight away and a "Jump to latest" pill appears above the composer. You can also type your own message or press the stop button.

Brand in motion

The logo is the loader

The Bhippi mark is two halves of a ring around a B, with gaps at the top and bottom. That split is a ready-made loader: the halves can open, turn, tick and shut. One motion family replaces the spinner, the pulsing avatar ring and the stop-button halo.

The mark is redrawn as a small SVG: two 17-unit stroked arcs with 9° gaps, a B path, and the logo's orange-to-rust gradient. Every state animates only transform on the ring group, the two halves and the B, so it stays cheap even at 16px. Under reduced motion it holds still, and the label beside it says what is happening.

Colour

One colour for Bhippi, one for you

The editor already uses blue for selection, focus and timecode, which are all yours. Bhippi takes the ember orange from its logo. Anything orange is Bhippi doing something. Anything blue is waiting on you. Green and red stay reserved for done and failed.

    Timer

    The seconds use tabular numbers so the row never jitters. The timer updates once a second in its own span, without re-rendering the whole message.

    Scrolling

    Scrolling that holds your place

    This is the ChatGPT and Codex pattern. When you send, your message glides to the top and the reply grows into space reserved beneath it. Bhippi then follows the live step gently. The moment you scroll up, it lets go.

    • On sendMeasure the new message's layout offset and put a reserve div after the reply, sized max(0, top + viewport − content). Glide there with a spring (damping 0.7, stiffness 0.05, mass 1.25, from use-stick-to-bottom). Never jump.
    • While workingHold the message at the top until the live line reaches the fold, then follow the live line with the same spring. "Hold at top" in the demo never follows, like the Codex default users asked for back.
    • Letting goAny upward wheel, touch drag or PageUp/Home lets go at once. Only an upward move where scrollHeight hasn't changed counts as you, so Bhippi's own scroll events can't unpin it.
    • Coming backA pill 10px above the composer (measured, so a tall draft can't cover it) shows "Jump to latest" or "2 new steps" with an ember dot while work continues. Reaching the bottom by hand re-attaches too.
    • FoldingFolds animate grid-template-rows, and the reserve is re-measured every frame, so a collapsing card never makes the view jump. The reserve stays until the next send, so the view never clamps back up.
    • Smooth vs instantSprings for sending, following and the pill. Instant for loading a thread and under reduced motion. overflow-anchor: none on the scroller, because the hook owns the position. WKWebView on macOS ignores anchoring anyway.

    Streaming

    Words fade in instead of popping

    Today the whole answer is re-parsed and replaced on every chunk, so text lands in lumps. Instead, finished paragraphs are memoised, only the last one re-renders, and each new word fades in from a 4px blur over 240ms. The stagger never falls more than 320ms behind the stream.

    Eight steps. Each one ships on its own and leaves the chat working. The first step is mostly deletion.

    2. BhippiMark component
